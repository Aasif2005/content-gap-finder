# YouTube Video Ideas for 2027: 45 Ideas Built Around What's Changing

*Video ideas for YouTube in 2027, matched to the six shifts that decide what gets watched next year.*

A video idea doesn't age because the topic gets old. It ages because YouTube changes around it. Videos that worked when Shorts were 60 seconds, when most people watched on phones, and when 4,000 watch hours got you paid were planned for a YouTube that's already gone.

I'm Aasif. I build a tool that reads comment sections to find what viewers ask for, so I spend a lot of time inside YouTube's numbers and announcements. Instead of another list of a hundred titles, here are 45 video ideas for YouTube in 2027, each tied to something that's actually changing next year. Find the shift that suits your channel and start there.

---

## Shift 1: The Partner Program bar doubles on 1 February 2027

From 1 February 2027, new channels applying for ad revenue need 1,000 subscribers plus **8,000** qualified watch hours in the last year, or **20 million** qualified Shorts views in 90 days. Today it's 4,000 hours and 10 million views. Channels already in the programme aren't affected ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)).

If you're not monetised yet, watch time matters more than it ever has. These ideas are built to earn it:

**1. A complete course in one video.** Example: "Learn to crochet: the full beginner course (2 hours)." One long, useful video can earn more watch hours than twenty short ones.

**2. A live build, edited down to an hour.** Example: "Building a raised garden bed from scrap wood, every step." People who start a long project video tend to want to see the finish.

**3. A numbered series that viewers binge.** Example: "Spanish for travellers, lesson 1 of 20." Series turn one viewer into twenty views.

**4. "Every question I've been asked about [topic]."** Example: "Every question about owning a rabbit, answered." It's long because the questions are many, not because you padded it.

**5. A re-made version of your best long video.** If one video already earns most of your watch time, a newer, better version for 2027 is the safest bet you have.

## Shift 2: YouTube is now a TV channel

In Nielsen's August 2026 report, YouTube took a record 15.8% of all US TV viewing time ([Tubefilter](https://www.tubefilter.com/2026/10/22/nielsen-the-gauge-youtube-record-watch-time-share-us-tv/)). People sit on a sofa and watch YouTube the way they used to watch cable. That favours different video ideas:

**6. A documentary-style story from your niche.** Example: "The rise and fall of the shopping mall in my town." Sit-back stories suit the big screen.

**7. A real-time "come with me" video.** Example: "Opening my coffee cart, 5 a.m. to close." The TV audience likes company more than cuts.

**8. A watch-together review.** Example: "Rating every Christmas film I watched this year, with my family." It feels like a show, not a clip.

**9. A scenic drive or train ride with light commentary.** Example: "The slow train through the Scottish Highlands, window seat." It looks good at 55 inches and holds viewers for a long time.

**10. A competition between friends, filmed like a game show.** Example: "Three friends, $30, best homemade pizza wins." Formats people know from TV feel natural on it.

## Shift 3: A Short can now tell a whole story

Since October 2024, vertical videos up to three minutes count as Shorts ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)). Most Shorts are still built for 30 seconds, which leaves the longer, story-shaped Short underused:

**11. A two-minute story with a twist.** Example: "The customer who paid in coins, all 4,000 of them." Beginning, middle, end, in one Short.

**12. A mini-tutorial that is actually complete.** Example: "Tie a bowline knot, three ways, in two minutes." Long enough to really learn it.

**13. A "speed-run" explainer of a big topic.** Example: "The entire history of the Roman Empire in 180 seconds." The time limit is the hook.

**14. A day compressed into two minutes.** Example: "48 hours as a wedding photographer, in 2 minutes." Enough time to show the hard parts too.

**15. A debate Short with both sides.** Example: "Gas vs induction hob: the honest case for each." Two minutes is just enough to be fair.

## Shift 4: Podcasts have moved to YouTube

YouTube says more than a billion people a month watch podcast content there ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/1-billion-monthly-podcast-users/)), and many of them watch on the TV. Ideas for a talk-based channel:

**16. A 30-minute conversation with someone in a job people wonder about.** Example: "An air traffic controller answers everything." Curiosity carries long talk videos.

**17. A "two experts disagree" episode.** Example: "Two personal trainers argue about cardio." Disagreement holds attention better than agreement.

**18. A solo episode reading and answering your comments.** Example: "I answer your hardest questions about van life." You already have the questions.

**19. A "where are they now" follow-up.** Example: "I called the people from my first video, three years later." Returning viewers love a sequel.

**20. A podcast clips channel.** Example: the best 90 seconds of each episode, posted as Shorts that link back to the full episode.

## Shift 5: Your videos can travel to other languages

Auto dubbing in YouTube Studio can now translate videos into many languages ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/creator-and-artist-stories/youtube-auto-dubbing-explained/)). That opens up ideas that travel well beyond your own country:

**21. "How [thing] works", shown on screen.** Example: "How a dishwasher actually cleans, with a clear panel." Visual explanations survive translation.

**22. A recipe from your home country, explained for outsiders.** Example: "Kerala fish curry for people who've never cooked Indian food." The curious audience is worldwide.

**23. A guide to your city for visitors.** Example: "Lisbon in three days without a car." Tourists search in their own language.

**24. A craft or skill with universal appeal.** Example: "Fixing a wobbly chair the right way." A wobbly chair is the same problem in every country.

**25. A science demonstration.** Example: "What happens to a phone at -40°C." Experiments need almost no words.

Tip: for videos meant to be dubbed, speak clearly, avoid slang and puns, and keep text off the screen where you can.

## For brand-new channels starting in 2027

**32. "Day 1 of my channel: here's the plan for the year."** Example: "I'm starting a woodworking channel with $200 of tools." A public plan gives early viewers a reason to return.

**33. The question you looked up last week.** Example: "How long does a car battery actually last?" If you searched it, others do too.

**34. Test a tip that's been around for years.** Example: "Does putting a wet phone in rice actually work?" Old advice is a free hook, and testing it is easy to film.

**35. A 30-day challenge, filmed from the start.** Example: "I practised piano 20 minutes a day for 30 days." January is when people start things.

**36. Your honest first-month numbers.** Example: "My first 30 days on YouTube: views, subs, and what flopped." Other beginners want to know what normal looks like.

**37. "The kit I'd buy with $100."** Example: "A $100 podcast setup that sounds expensive." A fixed budget makes the title concrete.

## Ideas that will still work in 2028

**38. The most-asked question in your comments, answered properly.** Example: "You asked how I price my pottery. Here's the exact maths."

**39. A redo of the best video in your niche from five years ago.** Example: "Re-making the most-watched sourdough video of 2021, with what we know now."

**40. "Everything I know about [topic] in one video."** Example: "Everything I know about keeping chickens, in 40 minutes." It becomes your channel's front door.

**41. Beginner vs. one year in.** Example: "My first week of calligraphy vs. my 365th." Progress is proof.

**42. The myth in your niche, tested.** Example: "Does charging your phone overnight really ruin the battery?"

**43. The tool you'd replace everything with.** Example: "If I could keep one kitchen knife, it's this one." A single strong opinion is easy to share.

**44. "I asked 100 people…"** Example: "I asked 100 runners what made them quit." Real answers from real people can't be copied.

**45. The behind-the-scenes of your own channel.** Example: "How long one of my videos actually takes to make." Viewers and other creators both watch.

## Pick by asking your viewers, not by guessing

Any of these can flop if it isn't something your audience wants. The fastest way to know is to look at what viewers are already asking:

- **Read the latest comments in your niche.** Questions that repeat across several videos are your best video ideas. Viewers are telling you what they want.
- **Type the idea into YouTube search.** Watch what YouTube suggests as you type. Those suggestions come from real searches.
- **Watch the top three videos on it.** If they're dated, too long-winded, or miss the point, there's room for yours.

If an idea fails all three, put it aside for later.
