# 40 YouTube Shorts Ideas for 2027 (Now That Shorts Can Be 3 Minutes)

*Good YouTube Shorts ideas sorted by length, from 15-second hooks to 3-minute mini-documentaries, plus ideas without showing your face and the 2027 Shorts money change.*

A lot of YouTube Shorts ideas online were written for a 60-second limit. That limit went away in October 2024, when YouTube started counting vertical videos up to **three minutes** long as Shorts ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)).

Length is now a choice, and different ideas need different lengths. A tip that takes 15 seconds feels padded at a minute; a story squeezed into 30 seconds loses its ending. So these 40 Shorts ideas are sorted by the length they need.

I'm Aasif. The tool I make reads YouTube comment sections, and under Shorts the most useful comments are the ones asking for more: "how?", "part 2?", "do one on…". I picked ideas that tend to invite those.

---

## What changes for Shorts in 2027

- **Huge audience, heavy competition.** YouTube's CEO said in 2025 that Shorts average more than 200 billion views a day ([TheWrap](https://www.thewrap.com/youtube-shorts-200-billion-daily-views/)).
- **A threshold for Shorts revenue.** From 1 February 2027, sharing in Shorts ad and subscription revenue needs 10 million qualified Shorts views in the last 90 days, and new channels joining the Partner Program through Shorts need 20 million ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)).

For most channels, then, a Short's main job in 2027 is to **bring in new viewers and send them on**: to a long video, a series, or your channel page. Give every Short a next step.

## Good YouTube Shorts ideas under 15 seconds

One hook, one payoff. Short enough that people watch it twice.

**1. The one-object answer.** Ask a question on screen, answer with a single object. Example: "What stops cut apple slices going brown?" (show the lemon).

**2. Wrong way, right way.** Show the common way, then the better one, with no talking needed. Example: "Stop peeling ginger with a knife. Use a spoon."

**3. The sound test.** Two products, one sound each, viewers vote. Example: two mechanical keyboards, typing the same word.

**4. The covered reveal.** Something hidden under a cloth, ten seconds of build-up, one reveal. Example: "What $5 of scrap wood became."

**5. One surprising fact, one image.** Example: "A teaspoon of a neutron star would weigh billions of tonnes." Check every fact against a reliable source before you post.

**6. The perfect loop.** End on the exact frame you started with, so it plays on seamlessly. Example: a pour that ends where it began.

**7. The three-second fix.** Example: "3, 2, 1: squeaky hinge fixed with a pencil."

**8. One number, one reaction.** A real, sourced figure from your niche, and your honest response to it.

## YouTube Shorts ideas for about 60 seconds

Long enough to teach one thing and prove it works.

**9. One mistake, fixed.** Example: "Why your homemade bread is dense, and the 10-second fix."

**10. A one-skill tutorial.** Example: "Fold a fitted sheet in 45 seconds."

**11. Cheap vs. expensive, tested.** Example: "$5 vs $50 frying pan: the fried-egg test."

**12. "I tried the viral hack."** Show the honest result, even if it failed.

**13. Myth or fact?** Example: "Does cracking your knuckles cause arthritis?" Put your source on screen.

**14. Answer a viewer, on camera.** YouTube lets you reply to a comment with a Short: the comment appears as a sticker and the commenter is notified ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12816427)).

**15. The messy middle.** Most before-and-afters skip the hard part. Show it, sped up.

**16. "What $20 buys you in…"** A city market, a hardware store, a supermarket abroad.

**17. A hidden feature.** Example: "Hold the space bar: your phone keyboard has a hidden cursor."

**18. A 60-second verdict.** Good, bad, buy or skip.

---

## 2 to 3-minute Shorts: room for a whole piece

The underused length. Two or three minutes is enough for a beginning, middle and end.

**19. A full recipe.** Ingredients, method, plating. Example: "Three-ingredient flatbread, start to finish."

**20. The history of one everyday object.** Example: "Why pencils are yellow."

**21. A mini-documentary.** One event, one person, one turning point. Example: "The bridge that collapsed because of the wind."

**22. Your first lesson on an instrument.** Example: "Three guitar chords, and a song that uses all three."

**23. Beat the clock.** Example: "Can I build a birdhouse in three minutes?"

**24. A place, walked through.** A shop, a trail, a museum room, filmed by you.

**25. The complete repair.** Example: "Replacing a phone battery, every step."

**26. A three-minute interview.** Three questions for someone interesting in your niche. Example: "Three questions for a beekeeper."

**27. Unbox to verdict.** Open, set up, test, decide, all in one Short.

**28. A mini-vlog with one goal.** Example: "Trying to find the best croissant in Paris before noon."

---

## YouTube Shorts ideas without showing your face

**29. Hands-only demonstrations.** Example: "Tying a tie, filmed from your point of view."

**30. Screen recording plus voiceover.** Example: "The Google Maps setting that saves battery."

**31. Point-of-view walks.** Camera at chest height. Example: "Walking through the night market in Taipei."

**32. Text over your own footage.** Example: a short story told in on-screen captions over clips you filmed. Keep the footage yours to stay clear of YouTube's rules on repetitive, mass-produced content ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/1311392)).

**33. Satisfying processes.** Pouring resin, slicing bread, cleaning trainers. Example: "Cleaning white trainers that were nearly black."

## Series ideas: how Shorts turn viewers into subscribers

One Short gets a view. A series gives people a reason to follow.

**34. "Part 1 of 10."** A build, a journey or a challenge, with the next part a day or two later.

**35. A question a day for 30 days.** Example: "Day 12 of answering your plant questions."

**36. "Rating every… in my city."** Coffee shops, parks, pizza places. Viewers nominate the next one.

**37. Learning a skill from zero.** A weekly progress Short. Example: "Week 9 of learning to juggle."

**38. A Short for every long video.** Cut the strongest moment and add a clear "full video" link.

**39. Viewer-chosen challenges.** Ask for suggestions, do the most-liked one next.

**40. A year-long project, weekly.** Example: "Restoring a boat: week 7."

---

## Testing a Shorts idea quickly

Shorts are cheap to make, so test instead of guessing:

1. **Check the comments on popular Shorts in your niche.** Repeated "how?" and "part 2?" comments show what viewers want more of.
2. **Decide the next step first.** If a Short doesn't lead anywhere (a series, a long video, your channel), it's a dead end even when it gets views.
3. **Post three hooks for one idea.** Same content, different first seconds, a few days apart. Keep the version that does best.
