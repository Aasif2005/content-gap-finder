# How to Find Trending Topics on YouTube in 2027 (The Trending Page Is Gone)

*YouTube removed its Trending page in 2025. Seven free ways to find trending topics on YouTube now, the topics already on the 2027 calendar, and how to tell a trend worth chasing from one that's over.*

Search "how to find trending topics on YouTube" and plenty of the advice still begins: open the Trending tab.

It isn't there any more. In July 2025 YouTube removed the Trending page and the "Trending Now" list, pointing people to YouTube Charts and the Gaming Explore page instead ([9to5Google](https://9to5google.com/2025/07/10/youtube-trending-page-removal/)).

I'm Aasif, and most of my week goes into building a tool that reads YouTube comment sections. Comments turn out to be one of the earliest places a trend shows up. Below is how I'd look for trending topics on YouTube in 2027, with free tools only, from the quickest method to the slowest.

## Why trending topics matter more on YouTube in 2027

In its 2027 Partner Program update, YouTube said it plans new incentives including "earnings boosts for starting and growing trends" ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)). The details aren't out yet, but it's a clear sign YouTube wants creators who start trends, not just follow them.

The same update raises the bar for new channels: from 1 February 2027, joining the Partner Program for ads needs 8,000 watch hours (up from 4,000) or 20 million Shorts views. A well-timed video on a rising topic can help a small channel get noticed.

## 7 free ways to find trending topics on YouTube

### 1. YouTube Charts and the Gaming Explore page

**What:** The successors to the Trending page. Charts list the most popular content by category (music videos, podcasts, trailers), and gaming has its own Explore page with popular gaming videos.

**How:** Check the one nearest your niche weekly. Don't copy videos. Note the **topics** that keep appearing.

**Limit:** Charts show what's already big, so the first wave of videos is already out. Use them to confirm a trend, not to spot it early.

### 2. Google Trends with the YouTube filter

**What:** Google Trends can show search interest on **YouTube** rather than Google.

**How:**
1. Go to trends.google.com and enter your topic.
2. Switch the "Web Search" dropdown to **"YouTube Search."**
3. Look at the past 90 days, then the past 5 years.
4. Under **"Related queries,"** choose **"Rising."**

Rising queries show what people have **started** searching on YouTube around your topic. The 5-year view tells you whether something is a real trend or a yearly pattern, like Christmas recipes every December, which you can plan for in advance.

### 3. The YouTube search bar

**What:** As you type, YouTube suggests searches based on what people actually look for.

**How:** Type your topic plus a letter, "home workout a…", "home workout b…", through the alphabet. Then try "why does…", "is it worth…", "how to… 2027".

**Look for:** suggestions naming something new (a product, a rule, an event). Those are early trend signs. Evergreen phrases like "for beginners" are steady demand rather than a trend.

### 4. The search terms in your own Studio

**What:** If you already have videos, Studio tells you what people typed to find them: in Analytics, under Reach, the "Traffic source: YouTube search" card lists the terms ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/9314355)).

**How:** Look monthly. A new term appearing on an old video hints that interest is growing. Make a newer, more focused video for it.

### 5. Comments under the newest videos in your niche

**What:** Where viewers ask for things before anyone has made them.

**How:** Open the ten most recent videos from channels in your niche and sort comments by "Newest." Watch for:

- the **same question** under different creators' videos;
- **new names**: a product, person, game or rule nobody mentioned last month;
- **"Can you do one on…"** requests.

When one new question keeps appearing across several channels within a few weeks, it's often an early sign of a trend, sometimes before it's obvious in search tools. Doing this by hand is slow, but it's the signal I trust most.

### 6. Popular sounds and formats in Shorts

**What:** In the Shorts camera, the sound library shows popular sounds, and tapping one shows the Shorts that use it.

**How:** Pick sounds that fit your niche and see what people make with them. A format that's popular elsewhere but missing from your niche is an opening.

**Limit:** Sound trends fade within days, so only chase them if you can post quickly. Need formats? See my [40 YouTube Shorts ideas for 2027]([LINK-04]).

### 7. The calendar

**What:** The most predictable trends: events you can see coming. More on 2027 below.

**How:** Publish two to four weeks **before** an event. Searches build in the run-up, and early videos are there to catch them.

## Is this trend worth chasing? Four questions

1. **Does it fit your channel?** This one is required. A trend outside your niche brings viewers who won't stay.
2. **Is it rising or peaking?** In Google Trends, a line still climbing is rising; a spike already falling is over.
3. **Are viewers asking questions about it?** Unanswered questions under existing videos mean room for yours. Comments like "we've seen this already" mean it's crowded.
4. **Will it matter in three months?** A trend that becomes lasting interest ("how to use the new…") beats a one-week meme.

If it fits your channel and passes at least two of the other three, film it this week.
