# YouTube Content Ideas for Beginners in 2027: Your First 10 Videos

*A video-by-video plan for a brand-new channel, the content ideas for YouTube that work when nobody knows you yet, and 20 more for when you run dry.*

The first video is easy: you've been thinking about it for weeks. The third is where the blank page arrives.

Lists of YouTube content ideas for beginners rarely help with that, because they treat every video as interchangeable. A new channel's videos aren't. With no subscribers, nobody is waiting for your upload, so your early videos have two jobs: **get found** by strangers, then give them a reason to watch a second one.

My name is Aasif. My work is building a tool that reads YouTube comments, and the questions under beginner-friendly videos are remarkably consistent from niche to niche. This plan is shaped by them: ten videos, in order, each with a job.

---

## What's different for new channels in 2027

From 1 February 2027, a new channel needs 1,000 subscribers and **8,000** watch hours in a year (double the old 4,000) to join the YouTube Partner Program for ads, or 20 million Shorts views in 90 days ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/youtube-partner-program-updates-2027-new-opportunities-earn/)).

Two habits help from day one:
- **Make videos people watch to the end.** As long as the topic needs, no longer: skip the slow intro, keep every minute useful.
- **Link every video to the next.** End screens, pinned comments and series titles turn one view into several.

## Videos 1–3: be findable

A brand-new channel has no subscribers to show videos to, so search is one of the few ways strangers can find you. These answer questions people already type.

**Video 1: Your niche's most basic question, answered better.**
Type your topic into YouTube search and note the suggestions. Pick the simplest. Example for a houseplant channel: "Why are my pothos leaves turning yellow?"

**Video 2: A guide for complete beginners.**
Example: "Houseplants for complete beginners: the first five to buy." People searching "beginner" want to feel capable, and they remember who helped.

**Video 3: Fix a problem.**
Something went wrong; you put it right. Example: "How to save an overwatered plant." Problems like this come up again and again, so the video stays useful.

---

## Videos 4–6: let viewers meet you

Search brings a trickle of viewers. Now give them a reason to come back for **you**.

**Video 4: Your honest view on a debate in your niche.**
Example: "Are self-watering pots worth it? My take after a year." Opinions make a channel feel like a person.

**Video 5: One project, start to finish.**
Example: "Turning one monstera into ten." People stay to see how it ends, which helps watch time.

**Video 6: Why you care.**
Your story, short and useful. Example: "I killed every plant I owned. Here's what changed."

---

## Videos 7–8: try Shorts and a series

**Video 7: Shorts cut from your first six videos.**
Take the best 30–90 seconds of an earlier video and post it as a Short pointing to the full one. Shorts can now run up to three minutes ([YouTube blog](https://blog.youtube/news-and-events/tall-updates-coming-to-shorts/)), so a whole tip fits.

**Video 8: Episode one of a series.**
Example: "Rescuing supermarket plants, episode 1." A series gives viewers a reason to come back for the next part.

---

## Videos 9–10: let your audience choose

By now you'll have a few comments. Many beginners skip this step and keep guessing.

**Video 9: Answer one of your own comments.**
Example: "You asked: is tap water OK for my plants?" You can also reply with a Short, which shows the viewer's comment on screen and notifies them ([YouTube Help](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12816427)).

**Video 10: Answer a question from a bigger channel's comments.**
Open the newest videos from larger channels in your niche, find a question several viewers asked that the video didn't answer, and make that video. People have already said they want it, and the bigger channel left the gap open. It's one of the best content ideas a small channel can find.

## 20 more YouTube content ideas for beginners

For the weeks when nothing comes to mind. Each works in almost any niche.

1. **"What I wish I'd known before starting [topic]."** Example: "What I wish I'd known before my first aquarium."
2. **Your setup and what it cost.** Example: "My whole baking kit, and what I'd skip."
3. **Beginner vs. pro, same task.** Example: "A beginner and a chef chop the same onion."
4. **A product you regret buying.** Example: "The $90 gadget I used twice." Honesty earns trust fast.
5. **The cheapest way to start.** Example: "Start drawing for under $10."
6. **Five mistakes beginners make.** Example: "Five mistakes that kill new sourdough starters."
7. **Two popular options, side by side.** Example: "Duolingo vs. a textbook for 30 days."
8. **A 7-day challenge.** Example: "Seven days of cold showers: what happened."
9. **What to do this month.** Example: "Garden jobs for March."
10. **What's in your bag, kit or kitchen.** Example: "Everything in my hiking pack, and why."
11. **A tour.** Example: "A tour of my balcony garden, one year on."
12. **A Q&A from your comments.** Ten questions, quick answers.
13. **An advice correction.** Example: "I was wrong about watering succulents."
14. **"How long does it really take to…?"** Example: "How long does it really take to learn to touch-type?"
15. **The second-most-asked question in your niche.** Most channels stop at the first.
16. **First attempt vs. latest attempt.** Example: "My first loaf vs. my hundredth."
17. **An interview with someone further along.** Example: "I asked a 20-year gardener my beginner questions."
18. **A myth, put to the test.** Example: "Does talking to plants help them grow?"
19. **"What's changing in 2027"** for your niche: new rules, prices or products.
20. **Remaking your very first video.** Example: "Re-filming my first video, one year later."

## Three mistakes that stall new channels

1. **Filming for subscribers you don't have yet.** "Welcome back, guys" vlogs work when people are waiting for you. Early on, give strangers a clear reason to click.
2. **Switching topics every video.** Viewers need to know what your channel is for. Stay on one topic for at least your first ten videos.
3. **Guessing instead of reading.** The comments in your niche show what viewers want. Read them before you plan, not after you upload.
